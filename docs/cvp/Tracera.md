# CVP — Tracera

## Definition

The Tracera CVP is the smallest **real, encapsulated subset of mature Tracera** that closes the canonical product loop without establishing architecture that must be rebuilt for MVP.

```text
accepted product + intent
 -> persistent hierarchy/graph
 -> trace/evidence binding
 -> deterministic assessment
 -> dissatisfaction finding
 -> bounded human/agent navigation
 -> external resolution reference
 -> re-verification
 -> truthful product-state update
```

## CVP rules

- CVP is a projection over the mature contract, not a separate product definition.
- The product model, identity, evidence and assessment boundaries should be mature-shaped.
- Breadth may be stubbed; the vertical spine must be real.
- Local/simple adapters are acceptable where later replacement cost is bounded and trivial.
- A work item closing does not equal product acceptance.
- Missing, stale, inconclusive, skipped or mismatched evidence cannot produce green.
- CVP completion must be derived from its required FRs, essential features and critical journeys.

## What CVP must make possible

A user/agent can:
1. establish/retrieve a durable product and accepted baseline;
2. navigate from product intent toward implementation/evidence through bounded graph semantics;
3. ingest evidence bound to the correct product/baseline/capability;
4. derive truthful assessment and dissatisfaction;
5. inspect why the finding exists;
6. reference resolution work without transferring work authority into Tracera;
7. ingest new proof and see the product state change only after re-verification;
8. view stage/product status without confusing scaffold breadth with usable closure.

## Growth requirement

The CVP SHOULD reach MVP primarily by adding/enriching features, adapters and journeys. Any CVP-only replacement must declare transition debt.

## Superseded CVP framing

The September 20 CVP centered session trace capture, cross-run memory and a specific Vercel/Cloudflare deployment path. Those can support Tracera but are no longer the canonical definition of product viability. Deployment topology is not product identity.
