# Portfolio non-negotiables

1. Every owner repository is inventoried and receives a terminal role or explicit unresolved decision.
2. Private, archived and fork repositories remain in evidence scope.
3. Raw repository count is separated from operational, authority, public-brand, incubator, fork and archive counts.
4. Richer local intent/prompt/provenance systems are preserved.
5. Human source text is distinct from LLM synthesis.
6. Current, historical and proposed behavior are never collapsed.
7. Cross-repository authority is centrally adjudicated.
8. Product-local agents cannot archive or supersede siblings.
9. Capability/component mapping precedes repo-level merge decisions.
10. Migrations preserve provenance, licensing, consumers, compatibility, releases and rollback.
11. A repository birth requires an explicit boundary or TTL incubation contract.
12. A public product needs a distinct job or evidence-backed wedge.
13. A strategic fork needs upstream and patch-governance documentation.
14. A quality suite must demonstrate that it can fail.
15. A release artifact—not only source—is required for product closure.
16. SOTA plans are source-backed; pilots use equivalent tasks and oracles.
17. Tradeoffs are allowed only when explicit; critical regressions are not hidden.
18. Work is closure-first with WIP limits.
19. Unknown and blocked are valid; invented evidence is not.
20. Public maps are generated audience-specific projections of canonical records.
