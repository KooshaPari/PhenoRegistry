# D11 — Visual and interaction polish

Status: authored candidate predicates. No product results or automatically qualified adapters are implied. Exact-duplicate checks do not establish semantic uniqueness; resolve cross-cutting overlaps when weighting an assignment.

## D11-P01 — Design system and visual coherence
Applicability trigger: products with a visual presentation.
Method: **SEMANTIC**. Inspect rendered examples across the selected product states; compare actual component usage to an accepted visual system and intentional exceptions.

- **PEP-11-01-01** — Repeated components use consistent visual semantics.
- **PEP-11-01-02** — Color roles distinguish emphasis, state and decoration.
- **PEP-11-01-03** — Typography establishes a discernible information hierarchy.
- **PEP-11-01-04** — Spacing relationships remain coherent across related screens.
- **PEP-11-01-05** — Corner, border and elevation treatments follow an intentional pattern.
- **PEP-11-01-06** — Icon meanings remain consistent across contexts.
- **PEP-11-01-07** — An intentional visual exception has a user-facing purpose.
- **PEP-11-01-08** — Dark and light presentations preserve the same semantic priorities when both are supported.
- **PEP-11-01-09** — Brand assets match the actual product identity.
- **PEP-11-01-10** — A design-token file alone is not accepted as evidence of rendered consistency.

## D11-P02 — Layout and responsive craft
Applicability trigger: visual interfaces across declared viewport or display sizes.
Method: **DYNAMIC**. Capture actual rendered states at the selected sizes and content extremes; assert geometry and inspect task-preserving behavior.

- **PEP-11-02-01** — Primary content has no unintended horizontal overflow at supported widths.
- **PEP-11-02-02** — Controls do not overlap under realistic long content.
- **PEP-11-02-03** — Important actions remain visible without accidental clipping.
- **PEP-11-02-04** — Layout changes preserve task hierarchy across supported sizes.
- **PEP-11-02-05** — Empty content does not collapse the page into a broken geometry.
- **PEP-11-02-06** — Dense content has an intentional spacing and grouping strategy.
- **PEP-11-02-07** — Display scaling does not blur or misalign critical interface elements beyond the accepted target.
- **PEP-11-02-08** — Text truncation provides access to essential hidden meaning.
- **PEP-11-02-09** — Split panes and resizable regions respect declared minimum usable sizes.
- **PEP-11-02-10** — Responsive behavior is verified in the actual runtime rather than a static design mock.

## D11-P03 — Interaction feedback and motion
Applicability trigger: interfaces with interactive feedback or animation.
Method: **DYNAMIC**. Trigger interactions under normal and delayed execution; inspect feedback timing, interruptions and reduced-motion behavior.

- **PEP-11-03-01** — An activated control provides timely perceptible feedback.
- **PEP-11-03-02** — Pressed and selected states reflect actual interaction state.
- **PEP-11-03-03** — Animations do not conceal required information during a task.
- **PEP-11-03-04** — A canceled transition does not leave the interface in an invalid state.
- **PEP-11-03-05** — Motion duration and easing follow the accepted interaction language.
- **PEP-11-03-06** — Repeated clicks during an animation do not queue unintended actions.
- **PEP-11-03-07** — Reduced-motion mode preserves task understanding without nonessential animation.
- **PEP-11-03-08** — Loading transitions do not cause avoidable disruptive layout shifts.
- **PEP-11-03-09** — Audio or haptic feedback is proportionate and user-controllable where present.
- **PEP-11-03-10** — A visual success transition occurs only after its required effect is established.

## D11-P04 — Content density and visual hierarchy
Applicability trigger: information-rich human interfaces.
Method: **SEMANTIC**. Review real representative data with task goals; distinguish deliberate density from hidden actions, weak hierarchy or decorative noise.

- **PEP-11-04-01** — The most important task information is visually distinguishable.
- **PEP-11-04-02** — Metadata is subordinate to the decision or action it supports.
- **PEP-11-04-03** — Dense views preserve readable grouping and alignment.
- **PEP-11-04-04** — Empty decorative space does not force unnecessary navigation.
- **PEP-11-04-05** — Long-form content remains scannable through meaningful structure.
- **PEP-11-04-06** — Tables distinguish headers, units and exceptional values.
- **PEP-11-04-07** — Status colors have labels or other redundant meaning.
- **PEP-11-04-08** — Competing calls to action have an explicit priority.
- **PEP-11-04-09** — Progressive disclosure does not hide mandatory information.
- **PEP-11-04-10** — Visual hierarchy remains coherent with realistic rather than idealized content.

## D11-P05 — Media, screenshots and product proof
Applicability trigger: subjects producing demonstrations or public assets.
Method: **STRUCTURAL**. Trace media to its capture or authorship source; compare claims with raw masters and verify allowed redactions and rights.

- **PEP-11-05-01** — Product screenshots originate from the actual rendered subject when presented as proof.
- **PEP-11-05-02** — Conceptual visuals are explicitly labeled as conceptual.
- **PEP-11-05-03** — Evidence masters are preserved separately from annotated demonstrations.
- **PEP-11-05-04** — Timing in a performance demonstration is not silently sped up.
- **PEP-11-05-05** — Sensitive information is redacted without falsifying the demonstrated outcome.
- **PEP-11-05-06** — Media records identify the product revision and capture environment.
- **PEP-11-05-07** — CLI products use authentic terminal or consumer evidence rather than fabricated application screens.
- **PEP-11-05-08** — Library demonstrations exercise a real consuming program.
- **PEP-11-05-09** — Asset rights and attribution requirements are recorded before publication.
- **PEP-11-05-10** — Marketing media does not depict unimplemented functionality as shipping.

## D11-P06 — Polish review and craft acceptance
Applicability trigger: products with subjective presentation quality.
Method: **SEMANTIC**. Use anchored review cases and actual task evidence; record concrete defects, disagreement and the effect of proposed changes.

- **PEP-11-06-01** — A polish verdict names specific observable strengths or defects.
- **PEP-11-06-02** — Reviewers distinguish personal preference from a violated accepted design constraint.
- **PEP-11-06-03** — Comparisons use the same content and task state.
- **PEP-11-06-04** — A rating has behaviorally described anchors.
- **PEP-11-06-05** — Reviewer disagreement remains visible until adjudicated or accepted as uncertain.
- **PEP-11-06-06** — The review includes loading, empty and error presentation.
- **PEP-11-06-07** — Cosmetic improvement does not excuse a broken interaction.
- **PEP-11-06-08** — A polished screenshot cannot compensate for an unusable actual journey.
- **PEP-11-06-09** — A proposed visual change identifies the user problem it addresses.
- **PEP-11-06-10** — The final presentation is checked at the actual delivery artifact.
