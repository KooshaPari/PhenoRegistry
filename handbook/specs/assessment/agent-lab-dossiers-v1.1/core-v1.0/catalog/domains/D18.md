# D18 — Viability and lifecycle

Status: authored candidate predicates. No product results or automatically qualified adapters are implied. Exact-duplicate checks do not establish semantic uniqueness; resolve cross-cutting overlaps when weighting an assignment.

## D18-P01 — Stage and slice maturity
Applicability trigger: all product lifecycle assessments.
Method: **SEMANTIC**. Evaluate the selected stage's conjunctive gates against actual current evidence; keep value evidence and readiness axes independently visible.

- **PEP-18-01-01** — Concept maturity does not imply implemented behavior.
- **PEP-18-01-02** — A feasibility prototype resolves a named technical uncertainty.
- **PEP-18-01-03** — An integrated prototype demonstrates the selected end-to-end controlled journey.
- **PEP-18-01-04** — A slice MVP delivers its minimum promised outcome to the defined beneficiary slice.
- **PEP-18-01-05** — Supported-product status includes the relevant maintenance and recovery obligations.
- **PEP-18-01-06** — Publication readiness is separate from product functionality.
- **PEP-18-01-07** — Operational readiness is separate from successful deployment.
- **PEP-18-01-08** — Value evidence is not inferred from engineering quality.
- **PEP-18-01-09** — One mature slice does not promote an unsupported broader product scope.
- **PEP-18-01-10** — Stage promotion identifies the exact evidence and assignment baseline supporting it.

## D18-P02 — Portfolio allocation and lifecycle choices
Applicability trigger: subjects receiving ongoing lab resources.
Method: **SEMANTIC**. Compare improve, experiment, narrow, merge, maintain, pause, pivot, retire and no-action alternatives under the actual mandate and resource envelope.

- **PEP-18-02-01** — Allocation decisions identify the outcome or uncertainty they fund.
- **PEP-18-02-02** — A low engineering score is not treated as proof of absent demand.
- **PEP-18-02-03** — A high engineering score is not treated as proof of beneficiary value.
- **PEP-18-02-04** — The decision considers integration and maintenance burden.
- **PEP-18-02-05** — A merge alternative examines unique capability and consumer dependencies.
- **PEP-18-02-06** — A pause can preserve research value without implying deletion.
- **PEP-18-02-07** — Pivot decisions identify the evidence that invalidated the previous thesis.
- **PEP-18-02-08** — Resource changes remain within actual delegated limits.
- **PEP-18-02-09** — Lifecycle choices have review and reversal conditions.
- **PEP-18-02-10** — Repeated noisy observations do not force uncontrolled oscillation between dispositions.

## D18-P03 — Retirement, migration and custody
Applicability trigger: subjects being absorbed, archived or removed.
Method: **DYNAMIC**. Inventory live consumers and retained assets, exercise migration, verify custody and perform only authorized lifecycle effects.

- **PEP-18-03-01** — Retirement identifies actual dependent consumers.
- **PEP-18-03-02** — Unique retained capabilities have a destination or explicit abandonment decision.
- **PEP-18-03-03** — Data custody is established before removing the original store.
- **PEP-18-03-04** — Migration preserves required identifiers and history.
- **PEP-18-03-05** — Consumer compatibility is tested at the migration target.
- **PEP-18-03-06** — The original remains recoverable until accepted cutover conditions are met.
- **PEP-18-03-07** — Archival permission is distinguishable from deletion permission.
- **PEP-18-03-08** — Public references point to the accepted successor or explain the retirement.
- **PEP-18-03-09** — Retirement receipts identify the action actually performed.
- **PEP-18-03-10** — An unused repository can be paused without falsely claiming full migration completion.

## D18-P04 — Economics and bounded resource use
Applicability trigger: agent-operated work with resource costs.
Method: **OBSERVATIONAL**. Collect actual worker, tool, runtime, setup, verification and rework observations; compare accepted outcomes under a declared budget.

- **PEP-18-04-01** — Resource accounting includes unsuccessful attempts.
- **PEP-18-04-02** — Verification cost is included alongside generation cost.
- **PEP-18-04-03** — Setup and environment-recovery effort are counted when material.
- **PEP-18-04-04** — Elapsed latency is reported separately from aggregate worker time.
- **PEP-18-04-05** — Cumulative task spending survives session replacement.
- **PEP-18-04-06** — An exhausted budget produces an explicit stop or reallocation decision.
- **PEP-18-04-07** — Economic claims distinguish observed costs from assumptions.
- **PEP-18-04-08** — The denominator is an accepted outcome or defined learning result rather than a rubric point.
- **PEP-18-04-09** — Parallelism benefits are compared with collision and integration overhead.
- **PEP-18-04-10** — New infrastructure work names a concrete consumer and observed blocker.

## D18-P05 — Convergence, stalls and restart policy
Applicability trigger: iterative agent execution.
Method: **OBSERVATIONAL**. Compare attempts under a fixed success definition; retain failure fingerprints, reopened defects and censored work, then classify stalls before intervention.

- **PEP-18-05-01** — Progress comparisons identify a stable assignment epoch.
- **PEP-18-05-02** — A repeated failure signature is retained across sessions.
- **PEP-18-05-03** — A stalled run is checked for environment failure before blaming implementation.
- **PEP-18-05-04** — A stalled run is checked for oracle defects using valid and invalid witnesses.
- **PEP-18-05-05** — A contract contradiction can trigger a new assignment epoch.
- **PEP-18-05-06** — Reopened defects are subtracted from naive closure counts.
- **PEP-18-05-07** — Restart, replay, reseed, replan and contract revision are separately recorded.
- **PEP-18-05-08** — Unsolved attempts remain in time-to-outcome analysis.
- **PEP-18-05-09** — The process does not claim eventual success solely from repeated attempts.
- **PEP-18-05-10** — A next action targets a discriminating observation rather than identical unbounded retries.

## D18-P06 — Lab autonomy and institution-level validation
Applicability trigger: the evaluation protocol and agent lab itself.
Method: **DYNAMIC**. Run a bounded no-platform vertical slice with agent-derived intent, qualified grading, an actual change or experiment, and a fresh-session handoff.

- **PEP-18-06-01** — The lab can begin authorized work without a human-authored local repository idea.
- **PEP-18-06-02** — A missing central platform does not block the file-first execution path.
- **PEP-18-06-03** — A fresh agent can reconstruct accepted state from retained records.
- **PEP-18-06-04** — The loop produces a real measurement or discriminating experiment rather than only new plans.
- **PEP-18-06-05** — A planted false pass is rejected through the actual verification path.
- **PEP-18-06-06** — Agent-generated opportunities do not automatically become mandatory backlog.
- **PEP-18-06-07** — Sponsor feedback is typed without turning every idea into an obligation.
- **PEP-18-06-08** — An agent can recommend stopping or merging its own project when evidence warrants it.
- **PEP-18-06-09** — The institution preserves learning when a worker is replaced.
- **PEP-18-06-10** — Lab-level assurance claims remain limited to the workflows and boundaries actually tested.
