# D13 — Reliability and operations

Status: authored candidate predicates. No product results or automatically qualified adapters are implied. Exact-duplicate checks do not establish semantic uniqueness; resolve cross-cutting overlaps when weighting an assignment.

## D13-P01 — Runtime observability
Applicability trigger: running products and services.
Method: **DYNAMIC**. Trigger known operations and failures; follow metrics, logs and traces to actual effects with bounded and redacted telemetry.

- **PEP-13-01-01** — Required operations emit an observable completion or failure signal.
- **PEP-13-01-02** — Telemetry identifies the relevant subject artifact and environment.
- **PEP-13-01-03** — A cross-component operation has usable correlation identifiers.
- **PEP-13-01-04** — Metrics distinguish availability from merely running processes.
- **PEP-13-01-05** — Structured logs preserve machine-readable error categories.
- **PEP-13-01-06** — Sensitive payloads are excluded or redacted according to policy.
- **PEP-13-01-07** — Telemetry failure does not corrupt the product's core operation.
- **PEP-13-01-08** — High-cardinality data is controlled under the operating budget.
- **PEP-13-01-09** — The operator can distinguish stale telemetry from healthy inactivity.
- **PEP-13-01-10** — A known injected failure is visible through the selected observability path.

## D13-P02 — Health, readiness and graceful degradation
Applicability trigger: deployable or continuously operated products.
Method: **DYNAMIC**. Exercise startup, dependency loss, recovery and shutdown; compare reported health with actual ability to fulfill required work.

- **PEP-13-02-01** — Liveness checks identify a stuck process when that is their claimed role.
- **PEP-13-02-02** — Readiness reflects actual ability to accept required work.
- **PEP-13-02-03** — Optional disabled features are distinguishable from failed required features.
- **PEP-13-02-04** — A degraded dependency is reported with the affected capability.
- **PEP-13-02-05** — Startup ordering does not expose a falsely ready endpoint.
- **PEP-13-02-06** — Readiness returns after recovery only when required behavior works.
- **PEP-13-02-07** — Graceful shutdown stops accepting new work at the correct point.
- **PEP-13-02-08** — In-flight work is completed or durably handed off during shutdown.
- **PEP-13-02-09** — Health checks do not themselves create destructive side effects.
- **PEP-13-02-10** — A green dashboard cannot override a failed core-journey probe.

## D13-P03 — Recovery, backups and restoration
Applicability trigger: stateful or operationally persistent products.
Method: **DYNAMIC**. Create representative data, produce a backup, inject an authorized failure and restore through the supported path; compare integrity and elapsed recovery.

- **PEP-13-03-01** — A backup contains the required recoverable data scope.
- **PEP-13-03-02** — Backup completion is verified beyond command exit status.
- **PEP-13-03-03** — A corrupt backup is detected before claiming successful recovery.
- **PEP-13-03-04** — A clean environment can restore the retained backup.
- **PEP-13-03-05** — Restored records preserve the required integrity predicates.
- **PEP-13-03-06** — Recovery time is measured against the accepted scenario budget.
- **PEP-13-03-07** — The declared recovery point matches the actually recoverable data age.
- **PEP-13-03-08** — Backup credentials and encryption material have a viable authorized recovery path.
- **PEP-13-03-09** — Restoration does not silently overwrite unrelated current data.
- **PEP-13-03-10** — The recovery exercise identifies its actual source and target versions.

## D13-P04 — Incident response and operational learning
Applicability trigger: operated products with consequential failures.
Method: **DYNAMIC**. Run a bounded incident drill from detection through containment, recovery and follow-up; verify role routing and evidence retention.

- **PEP-13-04-01** — A relevant alert reaches an authorized responder path.
- **PEP-13-04-02** — The alert identifies the affected user outcome or capability.
- **PEP-13-04-03** — The responder can locate a tested mitigation procedure.
- **PEP-13-04-04** — Containment actions remain inside actual operational authority.
- **PEP-13-04-05** — The incident record preserves an ordered causal timeline.
- **PEP-13-04-06** — Recovery is confirmed through product behavior rather than alert disappearance alone.
- **PEP-13-04-07** — A recurring incident creates an owned prevention or research action.
- **PEP-13-04-08** — Post-incident analysis distinguishes facts from causal hypotheses.
- **PEP-13-04-09** — Temporary mitigations have expiry or review triggers.
- **PEP-13-04-10** — An incident can proceed under delegated agent authority without unnecessary sponsor interruption.

## D13-P05 — Operations automation and change safety
Applicability trigger: products with automated operational actions.
Method: **DYNAMIC**. Exercise scheduled and triggered operations under valid, denied and partial-failure conditions; inspect idempotency, receipts and rollback.

- **PEP-13-05-01** — A scheduled operation records whether it actually ran.
- **PEP-13-05-02** — Retries use an idempotency mechanism appropriate to their side effects.
- **PEP-13-05-03** — Operational automation validates its target environment before mutation.
- **PEP-13-05-04** — A dry-run is distinguishable from a completed live action.
- **PEP-13-05-05** — Partial failure leaves a recoverable state and explicit receipt.
- **PEP-13-05-06** — Automated actions respect the selected maintenance or resource constraints.
- **PEP-13-05-07** — A failed permission check cannot be bypassed by fallback execution.
- **PEP-13-05-08** — A reversible operational change has a tested reversal path.
- **PEP-13-05-09** — Configuration drift is detected against the accepted baseline.
- **PEP-13-05-10** — A disabled automation does not continue through orphaned workers.

## D13-P06 — Continuity and long-lived service burden
Applicability trigger: products expected to remain operated or supported.
Method: **SEMANTIC**. Review actual service dependencies, expiry events, operating work and continuity scenarios; validate the highest-risk assumption with an exercise.

- **PEP-13-06-01** — Critical external service dependencies have identifiable failure consequences.
- **PEP-13-06-02** — Credential or certificate expiry has a renewal and detection path.
- **PEP-13-06-03** — The service can be transferred to another authorized operator or agent session.
- **PEP-13-06-04** — Recurring maintenance obligations have owners or automated execution.
- **PEP-13-06-05** — A loss of the original developer workstation does not destroy required operating knowledge.
- **PEP-13-06-06** — The support lifetime is consistent with available maintenance resources.
- **PEP-13-06-07** — Unfunded recurring dependencies are visible in lifecycle decisions.
- **PEP-13-06-08** — A provider exit or migration scenario has a bounded fallback plan.
- **PEP-13-06-09** — Required operational artifacts have retention and recovery policies.
- **PEP-13-06-10** — A continuity claim is limited to scenarios actually supported by evidence.
